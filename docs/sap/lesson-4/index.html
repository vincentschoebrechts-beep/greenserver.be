Planning with MRP

How SAP turns demand into proposals: what MRP calculates, the settings that drive it and the screen planners live in.

In this lesson

  • Understand the net requirements calculation.
  • Know the outputs of MRP: planned orders and purchase requisitions.
  • Read the stock/requirements list.

What MRP calculates

MRP (material requirements planning) compares demand with supply. Demand is made of sales orders and forecasts (planned independent requirements). Supply is the stock and everything already on its way: production orders, purchase orders. What is missing is the net requirement.

For each missing quantity, MRP creates a proposal at the right date. For a product made in-house, it creates a planned order and explodes the BOM: the components get dependent requirements, and MRP plans them too, level after level. For a purchased part, it creates a purchase requisition, which buyers turn into a purchase order.

The settings that drive it

They sit in the MRP views of the material master:

  • MRP type: whether and how the material is planned (PD for MRP, VB for a manual reorder point, ND for no planning).
  • Procurement type: E for in-house production, F for external procurement, X for both.
  • Lot size: EX orders exactly what is missing (lot-for-lot), FX a fixed quantity, HB up to a maximum stock.
  • Lead times: in-house production time, planned delivery time, goods receipt processing time. MRP schedules backwards from the date of need.

MRP Live and MD04

In S/4HANA the planning run is MRP Live (MD01N), which runs inside the SAP HANA database and is much faster than the classic run. Planners then work in the stock/requirements list (MD04, or the MRP apps in Fiori): stock, requirements and receipts in date order, with exception messages when something is late or excessive.

When the date comes closer, a planned order is converted into a production order: the proposal becomes a real order, ready to be released.

On the shop floor

Customers need 100 pumps next Friday. There are 20 in stock and nothing on order; the lot size is lot-for-lot. MRP proposes a planned order for 80 pumps, scheduled back from Friday by the production time. It explodes the BOM: 80 motors, 80 housings, 640 screws. Motors are bought, so they get a purchase requisition; housings are made in-house, so they get their own planned order.

Key points

  • Net requirement = demand − stock − receipts already planned.
  • In-house: planned orders, with dependent requirements for components. Bought: purchase requisitions.
  • MRP type, procurement type, lot size and lead times drive the result.
  • MD01N runs MRP Live; MD04 shows the live picture for one material.
