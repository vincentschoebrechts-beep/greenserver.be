Moving to SAP S/4HANA

Optional annex: why companies are moving now, the three ways in, how such a project runs, and what industrial IT should prepare on the shop floor side.

In this lesson

  • Know why so many companies are moving to S/4HANA now.
  • Tell a new implementation, a system conversion and a selective data transition apart.
  • Prepare the shop floor side of the project: interfaces, identifiers, cutover.

Why now

  • The end of SAP ECC. SAP's mainstream maintenance for ECC ends at the end of 2027. Optional extended maintenance, at a higher price, runs until the end of 2030. SAP's investment goes to S/4HANA, which SAP has committed to until at least 2040.
  • One system for the group. Groups often replace local ERPs, SAP or not, with one S/4HANA template, so that every site works and reports the same way.

Three ways in

ApproachWhat it meansTypical case
New implementation (greenfield)A new S/4HANA system built on standard processes; only selected data is migrated.The only way from a non-SAP ERP; also chosen to start clean.
System conversion (brownfield)An existing SAP ECC system is converted to S/4HANA, with its data and settings.A healthy ECC system, when history must be kept.
Selective data transitionA new system shell, with chosen data, companies or periods brought over.Groups merging systems or splitting a business.

The group template

In a group, a central team usually designs a global template: processes, settings and master data rules shared by all sites, then rolled out site after site. Each site adapts its local ways to the standard rather than the other way round: this is fit-to-standard. SAP also talks about a clean core: keep the SAP system as standard as possible and put company-specific extensions beside it, so that future upgrades stay easy.

How the project runs

Most projects follow SAP Activate, SAP's method, in six phases:

PhaseWhat happens
DiscoverUnderstand what S/4HANA offers and build the business case.
PrepareSet up the team, the plan, the systems and the governance.
ExploreFit-to-standard workshops: key users see the standard processes in a sandbox and list the real gaps.
RealizeConfigure, build interfaces and extensions, run test data loads, test everything together.
DeployTrain users, rehearse and run the cutover, go live.
RunHypercare right after go-live, then normal support and improvements.

Data migration

  1. Cleanse the old data: duplicates, obsolete parts, suppliers nobody uses anymore.
  2. Map old codes to new ones: material numbers, work centers, storage locations, units of measure.
  3. Load with the tools of the project, often the SAP S/4HANA Migration Cockpit, which works with one template per type of data.
  4. Check and sign off: business owners confirm that the loaded data is right. Several test loads come before the real one.

At cutover, open items move too: open orders, open purchase orders, and the stock, usually counted just before.

Checklist for industrial IT

  • List every flow between the old ERP and the plant: orders sent to the MES, confirmations, label printing, scanners, scales, quality results, maintenance counters. For each: direction, protocol, frequency, owner.
  • Check identifiers and field lengths. Production order numbers have 12 characters in SAP; material numbers can have up to 40 in S/4HANA. MES tables, HMI fields, PLC strings and barcodes sized for the old ERP may cut them.
  • Agree on storage locations and work centers with the project, so that what the floor sees matches what SAP knows.
  • Rebuild the interfaces with SAP's channels (IDoc, BAPI, OData) and test them end to end in the test system, with real scanners and printers.
  • Secure the new conduits: firewall rules, technical users, encryption, documentation (lesson 10).
  • Plan the cutover with production: reduced output or a stop, stock count, master data freeze, and a fallback plan if go-live is postponed.
  • Prepare hypercare: who to call, how interface errors are watched, how fast they are fixed.
  • Train key users and operators: screens, labels and scanner steps all change.

On the shop floor

A group decides to replace a site's local ERP with its S/4HANA template. Industrial IT lists fourteen flows with the plant. Testing shows that the MES expects 8-digit order numbers while SAP sends 12: the MES table and the label layouts are changed before go-live. After two test loads, the site goes live over a long weekend, right after a stock count. In the first week, thirty IDocs fail on a unit of measure mapping; hypercare fixes the mapping and reprocesses them.

Key points

  • ECC mainstream maintenance ends at the end of 2027; extended maintenance runs until 2030.
  • From a non-SAP ERP: new implementation. From ECC: conversion, new implementation or selective transition.
  • SAP Activate: Discover, Prepare, Explore (fit-to-standard), Realize, Deploy, Run.
  • For industrial IT: list the flows, check field lengths, rebuild and test interfaces, plan cutover and hypercare.
