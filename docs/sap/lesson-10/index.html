SAP from the shop floor · Lesson 10 of 10

Connecting and securing the shop floor

How data really travels between SAP and the machines, which channels exist, and how to keep that link from becoming a way into the plant.

In this lesson

  • Know the channels between SAP and the plant.
  • Follow a counter on the line all the way to a confirmation in SAP.
  • Apply basic security rules to the SAP link.

Translating between two worlds

A PLC knows tags, counters and events. SAP knows orders, confirmations and goods movements. Something in between translates: an MES, a gateway or a piece of middleware.

The channels

ChannelIn a few words
IDocSAP's format for asynchronous messages: sent, queued, processed later. LOIPRO is commonly used to send production orders to an MES.
BAPI over RFCFunctions in SAP that external programs call and that answer straight away, for example to post a confirmation.
OData APIsWeb services over HTTPS, documented on the SAP Business Accelerator Hub, such as the Production Order API and the Production Order Confirmation API.
SAP connectorsSAP Plant Connectivity (PCo) runs as a Windows service in the plant network and reads OPC UA servers, among others. With SAP Digital Manufacturing, SAP's cloud MES, the Production Connector plays that role.

From the counter to the confirmation

  1. SAP releases the order and sends it to the MES (for example as a LOIPRO IDoc).
  2. The PLC counts good and rejected parts; the MES reads the counters over OPC UA.
  3. At the end of the operation, the MES posts a confirmation with yield and scrap (BAPI or OData).
  4. SAP backflushes the components and posts the goods receipt.
  5. Failed messages are watched: BD87 shows IDocs by status and reprocesses them once the cause is fixed.

Securing the link

  • Zones and conduits. In IEC 62443 terms, the office network and the plant network are separate zones, and the SAP link is a conduit between them: it passes through a DMZ, it is filtered, and it is documented.
  • SAP never talks to the PLCs. It talks to the MES or the gateway; only those talk to the controllers.
  • One technical user per interface. Use a system or communication user, which cannot log on interactively, with only the rights the interface needs.
  • Encrypt. SNC for RFC, HTTPS for OData.
  • Watch. Failed IDocs and rejected calls are often the first sign of trouble, on either side.

On the shop floor

A plant connects its assembly lines to S/4HANA. The MES runs in the plant network and reads the PLCs over OPC UA. It exchanges data with SAP only through a server in the DMZ, with a dedicated communication user and SNC encryption. When the network team sees a new flow from the office network straight to a PLC, they know it is not the SAP link: that one never goes there.

Key points

  • An MES or gateway translates machine signals into business events.
  • IDoc: asynchronous. BAPI/RFC: immediate. OData: HTTPS APIs. PCo and the Production Connector read OPC UA.
  • Counter, MES, confirmation, backflush, goods receipt: no typing needed.
  • Zones and conduits, a DMZ, dedicated technical users, encryption and monitoring.
