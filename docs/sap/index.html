SAP from the shop floor

SAP S/4HANA explained to people who work with PLCs, supervision and MES. Ten short lessons in plain words, then a final quiz. Free, no account, nothing to install.

Who it is for

Automation engineers, maintenance and production staff, industrial IT people: anyone who knows the factory and wants to understand what SAP does with it, especially when a company moves to S/4HANA.

How it works

  • Ten lessons of about fifteen minutes each, to read in order or in any order.
  • Each lesson ends with three questions to check yourself.
  • The final quiz draws twenty questions from a bank of one hundred: two per lesson. Your score is calculated in your browser and is not sent anywhere.

The ten lessons

  1. SAP in the factoryWhat an ERP is, where SAP S/4HANA sits among the systems of a factory, and how SAP describes a company.
  2. Finding your way around SAPThe two ways of working in SAP, the transaction codes everyone uses, and the difference between master data and transactional data.
  3. Master data for productionThe material master, the bill of materials, the work center, the routing and the production version: the five records production relies on.
  4. Planning with MRPHow SAP turns demand into proposals: what MRP calculates, the settings that drive it and the screen planners live in.
  5. The production orderThe life of a production order from creation to closing, its statuses, and the goods movements and confirmations that the shop floor feeds.
  6. Stock and goods movementsHow SAP records every change in stock: movement types, stock types, documents, and the transactions the store uses every day.
  7. Quality managementInspection lots, results and usage decisions: how SAP QM checks what comes in, what is made and what goes to stock.
  8. Plant maintenanceHow SAP PM describes the installations, records breakdowns, plans repairs and triggers preventive maintenance, including from machine counters.
  9. Batches and traceabilityBatches, serial numbers and where-used lists: how SAP answers the question every recall starts with.
  10. Connecting and securing the shop floorHow data really travels between SAP and the machines, which channels exist, and how to keep that link from becoming a way into the plant.
