SAP in the factory

What an ERP is, where SAP S/4HANA sits among the systems of a factory, and how SAP describes a company.

In this lesson

  • Place SAP among the levels of a factory.
  • Know what SAP does, and what it does not do.
  • Recognise the main organisational units: client, company code, plant, storage location.

An ERP, in one sentence

An ERP (enterprise resource planning) keeps the whole company in one database: sales, purchasing, production, stock, maintenance, quality and accounting. When the store issues a part, the stock changes, the order's cost changes and the accounts change, all at once. SAP S/4HANA is SAP's current ERP. It follows SAP ECC, the previous generation, and runs only on SAP HANA, SAP's in-memory database. It can be installed on the company's own servers or used as a cloud service.

Where SAP sits

Factories are often described in levels, from the sensors at the bottom to the business systems at the top: this is the ISA-95 model. SAP lives at the top level.

SAP knows what must be made, from which parts, by when, and what it costs. It does not drive machines: it does not read a sensor every 10 ms, and it should never write to a PLC. Between SAP and the machines there is usually an MES (level 3) or at least a gateway.

The modules you will hear about

SAP is still described by its historical modules: PP (production planning), MM (materials management: purchasing and stock), QM (quality management), PM (plant maintenance), SD (sales and distribution) and FI/CO (finance and controlling). In S/4HANA they are one system sharing the same data, but the abbreviations are everywhere.

On the shop floor

A plant with two lines making pumps is plant 1100 in SAP. Raw materials sit in storage location 0001, the line-side racks in 0003, finished pumps in 0002. The PLCs and the supervision do not know any of these numbers: the MES, or the people, make the link.

Key points

  • SAP S/4HANA is an ERP: level 4, hours and days, not milliseconds.
  • It never drives machines; an MES or a gateway sits in between.
  • Client, company code, plant and storage location describe the company.
  • PP, MM, QM, PM, SD and FI/CO are the module names still in daily use.
